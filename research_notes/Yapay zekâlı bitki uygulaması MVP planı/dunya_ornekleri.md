# Global examples: AI-powered consumer plant apps (identification, diagnosis, care), state as of Oct 2026

Scope note: global benchmark of the leading apps for a Turkey-focused, subscription-only (no physical products) AI plant app MVP. Research date: 25 Oct 2026. App Store listing data was read on that date (US storefront unless noted). Third-party revenue and download estimates (Sensor Tower, AppSprint, ScreensDesign, Adapty) conflict heavily and are flagged as such. Where a figure comes from a search-engine summary because the page itself could not be fetched, that is noted.

## 1. Which apps lead, and what are their downloads, revenue estimates, ratings, and company backgrounds?

### Takeaway
PictureThis (Glority, Hangzhou) leads by a wide margin: about 1.1M App Store ratings, 50M+ Google Play installs, and reported company ARR of about $173M. Every other consumer plant app is at least an order of magnitude smaller. Planta, the leading pure "care" app, files about 85–96 MSEK (roughly $8–9M) in annual revenue. The rest of the field splits into four groups: Western/Eastern-European subscription apps (Plantum/AIBY, PlantIn/Genesis, Blossom/Bending Spoons, Greg), free non-profit or academic tools (Pl@ntNet, Seek/iNaturalist), agri apps that earn through input commerce (Plantix, Agrio), and Google Lens, a free, built-in rival with very large reach.

### Inferences
- Planta's filed revenue of about 85–96 MSEK/yr is roughly $8–9M at 2025 SEK/USD rates. That is about 1/20th of Glority's reported ARR. Even so, Planta is likely the largest Western pure care-scheduling app.
- The trackers' App-Store-only estimates (ScreensDesign $250K–450K/mo for Planta) undercount real revenue, probably because they miss Android and web. The report should treat tracker numbers as ordinal (rank) signals, not absolute ones.
- The market is a "winner + long tail": one ID-first giant, a few care-first apps with 100K–230K iOS ratings, and many template "AI plant identifier" clones earning $80K–200K/mo (see Q3).
- Plant apps are mostly filed under **Education** (PictureThis, Greg, Blossom, PlantIn) or **Lifestyle** (Planta). The RevenueCat Education and Lifestyle benchmarks are therefore the relevant comparators, not Health & Fitness.
- Turkish localization is a gap among the leaders. Only PictureThis and PlantIn list Turkish; Planta, Plantum, Blossom and Greg do not.

### Gaps
- No official 2025–2026 revenue figures for Glority, AIBY, PlantIn/Genesis or Bending Spoons' Blossom. Sensor Tower and Appfigures primary dashboards are paywalled.
- The Planta SEK figures could not be verified directly (allabolag/bolagsfakta/krafman returned 403).
- No 2025–2026 download totals for Seek or iNaturalist.
- No plant-specific figures for Google Lens or Gemini usage.
- The Plantix download numbers conflict (135M vs 40M) with no reconciliation.
- "Plant Parent" was not researched separately. Plantum's history (NatureID → Plantum) does not include that name.

## 2. What does each app do with AI, and how accurate are they?

### Takeaway
Every leader ships the same core stack:
- photo ID
- photo disease diagnosis
- a camera "light meter"
- a watering calculator or adaptive schedule
- reminders

They differ on human-expert vs AI-botanist consultation and on how much the schedule adapts. Independent tests consistently put PictureThis and Pl@ntNet on top for species ID: about 70–80% top-1 on houseplants and garden plants, about 47–59% on toxic plants (2020), and much worse on bark. General LLM chatbots are now competitive but make more confident errors. There is almost no independent validation of disease-diagnosis accuracy.

**Independent accuracy studies, species ID**
- **Toxic plants (peer-reviewed, 2020; 17 toxic species, iPhone).** Species identified 100% correctly: PictureThis 10/17 (59%, CI 36–78), Pl@ntNet 8/17 (47%), PlantSnap 1/17 (5.8%). — [PubMed 32794048](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32794048/)
- **Trees (peer-reviewed, 2022, Arboriculture & Urban Forestry 48(1); photos taken July 2020; 55 NJ tree species, 440 leaf and bark photos, 6 apps).**
  - PictureThis was most accurate: about 81% to genus and 68% to species combined. Leaves: 97% to genus, 84% to species. Bark: 65% to genus, 52% to species.
  - iNaturalist was second.
  - PlantSnap bark: about 0% to species.
  - No app exceeded 70% on bark.
  - The authors judge the apps reliable "to the genus level at best".
  - Source: [Schmidt et al., AUF 2022](https://auf.isa-arbor.com/content/48/1/27)
- **Garden plants (Michigan State University Extension, fall 2023; 177 photos).** PictureThis 73% correct, "reigning champion of this test for six years in a row"; Pl@ntNet third at 40%, behind PlantStory. — [MSU Extension](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/plant-identification-theres-an-app-for-that-actually-several) (page body failed to load; figures from search summary)
- **Houseplants (blog test, Apr 2026; 30 photos, blind first-answer grading).**
  - Results: Pl@ntNet 80%, PictureThis 77%, Google Lens 63%, Seek 60%.
  - Confident wrong answers: PictureThis 5, Pl@ntNet 3, Lens 4, Seek 2.
  - Pl@ntNet and PictureThis were both right 90% of the time when they agreed.
  - Caveat: the publisher promotes a sponsored "Grove AI Plant Buddy".
  - Source: [The Plant Index](https://www.theplantindex.com/articles/picturethis-vs-plantnet-vs-google-lens-accuracy-test)

**LLM accuracy**
- **Same blog, chatbots (2026, 30 photos).** Pl@ntNet 77%, Gemini 70%, Claude 67%, ChatGPT 60%. ChatGPT gave the most confident wrong answers (8). Not peer-reviewed. — [The Plant Index](https://www.theplantindex.com/articles/ai-chatbots-plant-identification-test)
- **Medicinal plants (peer-reviewed, 2026; 200 images, 4-option multiple choice, APIs accessed Oct 2025).** Qwen3-VL-235B 90.5%, GPT-4o 85.0%, Gemma3-27B 65.5%, Llama4-Scout 58.5%, DeepSeek-VL2 58.0%. — [Frontiers in Plant Science 2026 / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13350237/)
- **Disease and pest tasks (review).** Gemini-pro-1.5 led zero-shot (F1 50.45%); GPT-4o was best few-shot (F1 73.37%). Zero-shot was far weaker than fine-tuned models. — [ScienceDirect review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2643651525001670)

**Disease-diagnosis validation**
- Arxiv review (2022) of 17 disease-detection apps: most "cannot be used as a complete solution". Only Plantix combined ID, disease detection, a rich database and treatment suggestions. This was a quality review, not a diagnostic-accuracy test. — [arXiv 2208.02446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.02446)
- The "98% accuracy" claims for Plantix and PictureThis that circulate on review sites trace to marketing, not to published studies. — [search synthesis; e.g. greentowerharvest](https://greentowerharvest.com/blog/best-free-app-to-diagnose-plant-problems/)

### Inferences
- Species ID is commoditized:
  - Free tools (Pl@ntNet, Lens, Seek, and now ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude) come within about 10–20 points of PictureThis.
  - ID accuracy alone is therefore not a defensible paywall for a Turkish MVP.
  - The paywall value has to come from diagnosis plus treatment, an adaptive care plan, and conversation ("what should I do next?").
- The "confident wrong answer" metric matters more for UX than raw accuracy. Showing top-3 candidates with confidence scores, and asking for a second photo (leaf, flower, whole plant), reduces harm. Leaf-vs-bark gaps show that a multi-organ prompt helps.
- Diagnosis accuracy is essentially unvalidated industry-wide. An MVP that is honest about uncertainty and adds a feedback loop on "was this diagnosis right?", as PictureThis does, can differentiate.
- Human-expert consultation (PictureThis, PlantIn, Plantum) is a cost centre and a review-complaint driver when it is priced separately. An LLM "botanist" (Blossom) is the cheaper modern substitute.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed 2024–2026 head-to-head of the commercial apps (PictureThis, Plantum, PlantIn, Blossom, Planta) on species ID.
- No independent accuracy data at all for disease diagnosis on houseplants.
- No published accuracy for light meters or watering calculators.
- No benchmark covering plants native or common in Turkey.

## 3. Subscription models: free-tier limits, trials, prices, paywall type and placement, conversion and churn data

### Takeaway
The category standard is:
- a soft paywall shown immediately after a short onboarding, or even before the first scan
- a 7-day free trial, often with a "remind me before trial ends" toggle
- a yearly plan as the anchor (about $30–48 US, ₺799.99 for PictureThis in Turkey)
- monthly and/or weekly plans as decoys, sometimes a lifetime option

The free tiers are deliberately thin: a few IDs, plant-count caps, and premium-only diagnosis, light meter or fertilizing. No plant-specific conversion data is public. RevenueCat category benchmarks (Education/Lifestyle, MEA region) are the best proxies.

## 4. Retention features and review sentiment: what users praise and what complaints dominate

### Takeaway
Retention is built around:
- a "My Plants" garden with per-plant adaptive watering and fertilizing reminders
- a care history/journal
- weather-aware adjustments
- a vacation mode
- community feeds (Planta, Greg)

Users praise ID quality and helpful reminders. The dominant complaints are about billing, not AI quality:
- free trials that auto-convert to yearly or weekly charges
- cancellation that is not obvious
- "free" versions that do little
- human-botanist consultations sold on top of the subscription

### Cited Findings

**Retention mechanics in 2026 listings and teardowns**

### Inferences
- **Trust as a differentiator in Turkey**
  - Explicit trial-end reminders; showing the price in ₺ per month next to the yearly price; one-tap cancel instructions in the app; no weekly auto-renew trap.
  - This attacks the #1 complaint across the category.
- **What drives retention**
  - The durable loop is "My Plants" + adaptive reminders + a photo history and journal (growth over time).
  - Community feeds help the care-first apps (Planta, Greg) but add moderation cost (Greg is rated 16+).
  - For an MVP, a lightweight progress-photo timeline and seasonal and weather adjustments are cheaper than a full social feed.
- **Freemium value**
  - Locking reminders behind the paywall (Greg) produces "free version useless" reviews.
  - A healthier freemium cut: free ID with limited daily scans, plus basic reminders for a few plants. Premium: unlimited scans, diagnosis and treatment plan, AI chat, light meter, multi-plant smart schedule.
- **Consultation pricing**
  - Selling human consultations on top of a subscription generates resentment. Including an AI botanist chat in the subscription avoids this.

## 5. What do 2025–2026 newcomers do differently (LLM assistants, AR, on-device models)?

### Takeaway
The 2025–2026 wave is defined by LLM chat: "AI Plant Doctor", "AI Botanist" and per-plant chatbots. Other threads:
- chat-based plant personas (research)
- wearable and AR capture (Planta with Meta AI glasses; AR light meters)
- offline on-device models (Seek, PlantIn's offline mushroom ID)
- general assistants (ChatGPT, Gemini, Lens) becoming the free substitute

Incumbents have bolted on AI branding ("Planta: AI Plant & Garden Care", "Plantum – AI Plant Identifier", "Blossom – AI Plant Identifier"). Only a few have shipped a true conversational assistant.

### Inferences
- The App Store IDs of PlantDaily (id669…) and AI Plant Doctor (id674…) fall in the range issued in 2024–2025. That fits the claim that the LLM-chat wave is a 2024–2026 phenomenon. This is inferred from ID numbering, not from confirmed launch dates.
- **Recommended MVP positioning for Turkey**
  - Combine (a) a specialized vision ID/diagnosis model or API, for accuracy, with (b) an LLM conversational layer in Turkish that turns results into an actionable, adaptive care plan and follow-up ("send me a photo in 5 days").
  - Neither free LLMs alone (more confident errors, no reminders or memory of the user's plants) nor the incumbents (weak or no Turkish, chat sold as human add-on) cover this well.
  - Planta, Plantum, Blossom and Greg do not list Turkish at all.
- **Costs and positioning risks**
  - On-device and offline is a privacy and cost lever (Seek), but not a monetization differentiator.
  - LLM inference costs per chat turn make scan and chat quotas on the free tier necessary.
  - AR and wearables are marketing-led and low priority for an MVP.
